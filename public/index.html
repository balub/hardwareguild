Why we exist

India is full of engineers, makers, students, researchers, and tinkerers building genuinely interesting things.

But building hardware in the open is still hard. Finding collaborators is hard. Finding the right know-how is hard. Keeping a project alive once the first prototype works is hard.

We want a community where people can find each other, learn together, work on projects together, and add to an open hardware ecosystem that anyone can use.

This isn't just about meetups. It's about getting more open-source hardware built.

What we do

Community

Meetups, workshops, discussions, and local gatherings for people who like building hardware.

Build

Open-source hardware projects, built together. Find contributors, share ideas, and get your hands dirty on someone else's board.

Learn & share

Open documentation, tutorials, and shared know-how. Hardware should feel approachable, especially if you're new.

Support

Help open hardware projects get seen, find collaborators, and meet people who can help them move forward.

We don't hand out grants, equipment, or manufacturing services. What we have is people, time, and a lot of enthusiasm.

How we operate

Community-driven for real. Not as branding.

These are the principles we're holding ourselves to. We haven't built the formal processes behind them yet. We want to figure those out with the people who show up.

  1. 01

    Community voice

    Members should know how to suggest ideas, raise concerns, and influence where we're headed.

  2. 02

    Transparency

    Important decisions, priorities, and how things get done should be understandable and easy to find.

  3. 03

    Volunteer ownership

    If you take something on, you get real responsibility and a real say in how it's done.

  4. 04

    Open participation

    Anyone can contribute, whatever their background or experience.

  5. 05

    Shared direction

    The community shapes the goals and priorities. Organizers and volunteers turn them into action.

  6. 06

    Accountability

    Who's responsible for what should be clear. New people should get room to lead, so the same few don't run everything forever.

About us

We started as Absurd Industries, a small bunch of hardware enthusiasts in Bangalore who wanted to meet other makers and build things together.

Over time we saw a chance to focus on something more specific: helping open-source hardware grow in India.

So we're carrying the people, the community, and the volunteer spirit of Absurd forward, under a new name and a clearer mission.

We're still small. No big organization, no serious money behind us. We're building this alongside whoever chooses to show up.
